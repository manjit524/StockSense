Understanding Fixed Income & Sovereign Debt

Inverse Price-Yield Relationship

Bond prices move in the exact opposite direction of bond yields. When central banks cut interest rates, newly issued bonds yield less, causing existing higher-yielding bonds to surge in market price.

RBI Retail Direct Scheme

Retail investors in India can open a free Retail Direct Gilt (RDG) account directly with the Reserve Bank of India to purchase primary G-Secs, State Development Loans (SDLs), and T-Bills with zero brokerage.

US 10-Year Global Benchmark

The US 10-Year Treasury yield is considered the global "risk-free rate." When this yield spikes, capital flows back into USD assets, putting downward pressure on emerging market equities and currencies.

Sovereign Guarantee & Credit Spread

Government securities (G-Secs) carry zero credit default risk because they are backed by the sovereign authority. Corporate bonds offer higher yields to compensate investors for credit risk (credit spreads).

Frequently Asked Questions on Bonds & Yields

What is an Inverted Yield Curve and why does it matter?

An inverted yield curve occurs when short-term interest rates (like 2-Year Treasuries) are higher than long-term interest rates (like 10-Year Treasuries). Historically, an inverted yield curve has been one of the most reliable leading indicators of an upcoming economic recession.

What is the difference between Coupon Rate and Yield to Maturity (YTM)?

The coupon rate is the fixed annual interest paid by the issuer on the bond's face value (e.g. 7% on ₹100). Yield to Maturity (YTM) is the total anticipated annual return if you buy the bond at its current fluctuating market price and hold it until maturity.

How can retail investors buy Indian Government Securities?

You can buy G-Secs either through the RBI Retail Direct portal (online direct with RBI) or through major discount brokers (like Zerodha Coin, Groww) via non-competitive bidding during weekly auctions.

How are bond returns and interest taxed in India?

Interest received from government securities and corporate bonds is added to your total income and taxed at your applicable slab rate. Capital gains on listed bonds held > 12 months are taxed as LTCG at 12.5%.
